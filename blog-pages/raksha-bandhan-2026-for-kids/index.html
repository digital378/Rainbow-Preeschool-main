History

History & Origin of Raksha Bandhan

Raksha Bandhan is a very old Indian festival — it has been celebrated for thousands of years, and children love learning about the stories behind it. Its roots stretch back through ancient itihasa and puranic legends passed down through generations, long before it became the joyful family celebration we know today. Here are three of the best-loved stories:

Indra & Shachi

Long ago, the goddess Shachi tied a sacred thread on the wrist of Lord Indra, king of the sky, before a great battle. The thread gave him strength, and he won — one of the oldest tellings of the rakhi story.

Draupadi & Krishna

Children's favourite: Draupadi tied a piece of cloth on Lord Krishna's wounded finger. Touched by her kindness, Krishna promised to always protect her — and he kept his promise.

Karnavati & Humayun

A brave queen, Karnavati, once sent a rakhi to Emperor Humayun, asking him to be her brother and protect her kingdom. He respected the rakhi and came to her aid.

Even today, the rakhi thread carries the same power — not a magical power, but the power of love, trust and a heartfelt promise.

Meaning

Significance — What Does Raksha Bandhan Mean?

  • It celebrates siblings: the festival of brothers and sisters, reminding everyone to love and care for one another.
  • It is about promises: the rakhi isn't just a thread — it's a promise. Sisters pray for their brothers, and brothers promise protection.
  • It brings families together: families share food, exchange gifts, and celebrate their love.
  • It celebrates kindness: the spirit of the festival teaches children to be kind and caring — to siblings, friends, animals and even trees.
  • It is for everyone: sisters tie rakhis on sisters, children tie rakhis on trees — the festival belongs to anyone who believes in love and care.
Family Guide

How to Celebrate Raksha Bandhan — Step by Step

  1. Everyone wakes up early, takes a bath, and wears new or festive clothes.
  2. The sister arranges a puja thali with a rakhi, red roli powder, rice grains (akshat), a small diya, and sweets.
  3. The sister puts a red tilak on her brother's forehead to bless him.
  4. She ties the colourful rakhi on his right wrist while saying a prayer.
  5. She waves the diya (aarti) around his face, wishing him health and happiness.
  6. Everyone eats delicious sweets — ladoo, barfi or gulab jamun!
  7. The brother gives his sister a gift as a token of his promise.
  8. The whole family celebrates together with laughter, stories and happy memories.

For School

Short Essay on Raksha Bandhan

~150 words

Raksha Bandhan is one of India's most heartwarming festivals. It celebrates the loving bond between brothers and sisters. On this special day, sisters tie a colourful thread called a rakhi on their brothers' wrists and pray for their happiness and long life. Brothers, in turn, promise to always love, care for, and protect their sisters. The sister often applies a tilak on her brother's forehead and waves a small lamp before tying the rakhi. Families dress in new clothes, share delicious sweets like ladoo and barfi, and exchange gifts and warm wishes. Raksha Bandhan falls every year on the full moon day of the Hindu month of Shravana. At Rainbow Preschool International, Thane, our little ones celebrate this festival with joyful craft activities and stories. It is truly a day full of love, sweets, and smiles!

Story Time

Story for Kids: The Sister and the Thread

Long, long ago, in a faraway kingdom, a sister loved her brother very much. Every day, she prayed that her brother would always be happy, healthy, and safe. One day, she took a beautiful colourful thread and tied it on her brother's wrist — with all her love and all her prayers woven into it.

The brother looked at the thread and felt his sister's love. He said, "Sister, I promise — wherever I go, I will always look after you."

From that day on, every year on the full moon day of the month of Shravana, sisters and brothers all over India tie the rakhi and make their promise again. And that is how Raksha Bandhan became our favourite festival!

बहुत समय पहले, एक दूर के राज्य में, एक बहन अपने भाई से बहुत प्यार करती थी। वह हर दिन प्रार्थना करती थी कि उसका भाई हमेशा खुश, स्वस्थ और सुरक्षित रहे। एक दिन, उसने एक सुंदर रंगीन धागा लिया और अपने भाई की कलाई पर बांध दिया — उसमें उसका सारा प्यार और सारी दुआएं थीं।

भाई ने धागे को देखा और अपनी बहन का प्यार महसूस किया। उसने कहा, "बहन, मैं वादा करता हूं — मैं जहां भी जाऊं, हमेशा तुम्हारा ख्याल रखूंगा।"

उसी दिन से, हर साल श्रावण महीने की पूर्णिमा को, पूरे भारत में बहनें और भाई राखी बांधते हैं और अपना वादा फिर से दोहराते हैं। और इस तरह रक्षाबंधन हमारा सबसे प्यारा त्योहार बन गया!

फार पूर्वी, एका दूरच्या राज्यात, एक बहीण आपल्या भावावर खूप प्रेम करत होती. ती रोज प्रार्थना करायची की तिचा भाऊ नेहमी आनंदी, निरोगी आणि सुरक्षित राहावा. एके दिवशी, तिने एक सुंदर रंगीत धागा घेतला आणि तो आपल्या भावाच्या मनगटावर बांधला — त्यात तिचे सर्व प्रेम आणि सर्व प्रार्थना विणलेल्या होत्या.

भावाने तो धागा पाहिला आणि बहिणीचे प्रेम अनुभवले. तो म्हणाला, "बहिणी, मी वचन देतो — मी कुठेही गेलो तरी नेहमी तुझी काळजी घेईन."

त्या दिवसापासून, दरवर्षी श्रावण महिन्यातील पौर्णिमेला, संपूर्ण भारतात बहिणी आणि भाऊ राखी बांधतात आणि आपले वचन पुन्हा देतात. आणि अशा प्रकारे रक्षाबंधन आपला सर्वात आवडता सण बनला!

DIY, Ages 3–6

Rakhi Making Craft

Making a rakhi is one of the most joyful activities for young children during Raksha Bandhan. Here's a simple, fun craft for little ones to try at home or in school.

What You Need

  • Thick card / old greeting card
  • Child-safe scissors
  • Sketch pens or crayons
  • Glue stick
  • Glitter, sequins, stickers
  • Ribbon or thick thread

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Draw a circle, heart or star shape on the card — this is the face of your rakhi! (Adults can help very young children with this step.)
  2. Cut out the shape carefully (ages 3–4 will need an adult's help with cutting).
  3. Colour the shape brightly with your favourite colours.
  4. Decorate with glitter, sequins, stickers or foam shapes — be as creative as you like!
  5. Wait for the glue to dry completely.
  6. Punch a small hole at the top, or attach a ribbon with glue.
  7. Thread the ribbon through the hole, or glue it directly to the back.
  8. Your handmade rakhi is ready! Tie it lovingly on someone's wrist.

Teacher tip: Try a "class rakhi" — a paper strip with every child's handprint — a beautiful collaborative project celebrating friendship.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Raksha Bandhan 2026 falls on Friday, August 28, 2026, the full moon (Purnima) day of the Hindu month of Shravana.

Raksha Bandhan 2027 falls on Monday, August 16, 2027, also observed on the Shravana Purnima.

Raksha Bandhan is a special festival where sisters tie a beautiful thread called a rakhi on their brothers' wrists. Brothers promise to always take care of their sisters. Then everyone eats sweets and celebrates together — it's a festival of love and promises between siblings.

Keep it simple: "Today is a special day! Sister or brother is going to put a pretty bracelet on your wrist. It means we love each other very much!" Let toddlers enjoy the colours, sweets and festive atmosphere — they'll feel the joy even without understanding every ritual.

Narali Purnima is Maharashtra's festival on the same day as Raksha Bandhan. "Narali" means coconut — fishing communities offer coconuts to the sea, praying for safe waters and a good fishing season.

Preschools can celebrate with rakhi-making craft activities, simple storytelling about siblings and friendship, a "kindness circle", festive dress-up, nursery rhymes, and sharing homemade treats — with the focus on love, friendship and caring for each other.

Absolutely. Today, Raksha Bandhan is celebrated beyond the traditional brother-sister bond — sisters tie rakhis on sisters, friends tie rakhis on friends, and children tie rakhis on trees. It's about love, care and the promise of protection in every relationship.

A favourite child-friendly story is from the Mahabharata: Draupadi tied a piece of her saree on Lord Krishna's wounded finger to stop the bleeding. Touched by her kindness, Krishna promised to always protect her — and he kept his promise. It teaches children that kindness earns loyalty, and promises must be kept.

Closing Thoughts

Conclusion

Raksha Bandhan is more than a thread tied on a wrist — it is a promise, a memory, and a lesson in love that children carry with them for life. Whether your little one is tying their first rakhi, reciting a speech, or simply enjoying the sweets, every part of the celebration teaches warmth, kindness, and togetherness. From all of us at Rainbow Preschool International, Thane — may your Raksha Bandhan 2026 be filled with love, laughter, and the sweetest of memories.
